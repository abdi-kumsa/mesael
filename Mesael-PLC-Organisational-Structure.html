Mesael Construction PLC — Proposed Organisational Structure

Governance, departments, responsibilities and approval authority · Prepared by Abdi Kumsa · 10 August 2026
Proposal v1.0 — for CEO review

What this proposes, in one paragraph

Mesael is moving from a sole proprietorship to a Private Limited Company. That is not a change of name — it is a change of how the company is governed, who is accountable for what, and where the limits of each person's authority sit. This document proposes a two-tier executive structure: a Chief Executive Officer accountable to a Board of Directors, and a Deputy CEO who runs the company day to day through five departments. It gives a written responsibility list for every box on the chart, and an approval matrix that says exactly who may commit the company's money and up to what amount.

The design goal is the one the CEO named himself: he should stop being the bottleneck for routine work, while remaining unmistakably the highest authority on everything that matters. The structure below does both.

Why two bodies rather than one

The CEO's suggestion was that all department heads sit on the Board, with Waqjira as an external member. That instinct — give the heads a real, formal seat — is right, and it is delivered in full by the Management Committee above, which is the body that actually runs the company.

It is kept separate from the Board for one reason: the Board's job is to hold management accountable. If management is the Board, nobody supervises management, and banks, clients and the external auditor will all read the structure as governance in name only. A small Board — the CEO as Chair, the Deputy CEO, and Waqjira as the independent outside voice — keeps that accountability real while costing nothing in speed. If the CEO still prefers all heads on the Board itself, this is a one-line change.

Set the ceiling honestly

Reserving major payments for the CEO is the right instinct and it is built in above. But the whole purpose of the reorganisation is that the CEO stops being the constraint on routine work. If the ETB 500,000 line is set too low, every payment reaches him again and nothing has changed except the drawing. Set it where roughly four out of five payments by count clear below it — the CEO still sees every large sum, and stops seeing the small ones. He remains the highest authority; he just stops being the queue.

5 · Controls

Seven rules that must survive any simplification

non-negotiable

Grouping functions into fewer departments is fine. Grouping them into fewer people is not. These seven hold at every company size.

#RuleWhy — what it prevents
1The buyer never signs the goods receiving note.Otherwise one person can order goods, declare them received, and trigger payment for a delivery that never arrived.
2The person who approves a payment never releases it.Two hands on every Birr leaving the company. The bank mandate must require two signatories.
3Cost control does not report to the site.The person reporting an overspend cannot report to the person causing it. This is what pays for merging Operations and Engineering into one department.
4Internal Audit reports to the Board, never into the executive line.An auditor cannot audit their own manager. Without this, every other control here is unverified.
5Safety sits outside the project line.A safety officer reporting to the manager whose schedule safety delays cannot enforce anything.
6Nobody outside Finance handles company money.Ends reception being sent to the bank with urgent payments, and ends the purchaser paying garages by mobile banking — both currently outside every control.
7No payment is approved without its required documents attached.Reverses the single largest finding in the finance analysis: money currently moves first and the paperwork is chased afterwards, which is why the books run one to two months behind.

7 · Legal form

Before the conversion is announced

verify with counsel

The conversion is not a rename — and it carries a live commercial risk

Ethiopian practice treats a sole proprietorship to PLC conversion as closure and re-registration: tax clearance for the proprietorship first, then a new Memorandum of Association, new trade licence and new registration for the PLC. The consequence that matters commercially is this — the contractor grade, the prequalification history and the existing bank guarantees do not automatically follow the new entity. For a contractor that bids public work, that can mean being locked out of tenders during the transition, and having to re-establish a grade built up over years.

Sequence the conversion around the tender calendar, and get an Ethiopian corporate lawyer engaged before the structure is announced internally. This document is a management proposal, not legal advice.

PointPosition, to be confirmed with counsel
ShareholdersA PLC takes 2 to 50 members. A single-member PLC is possible under the 2021 Commercial Code. Decide whether Mesael holds it alone or brings in family or partners — this determines whether the Board is a real body or a formality.
Management modelThe Code allows either a board of directors (3–7 members) or a general manager appointed by the shareholders' meeting. This proposal assumes the board model, which is what makes the governance layer above possible.
External auditorMandatory for a PLC with ten or more members or total assets above ETB 10 million. Mesael is well above that threshold.
LiabilityMembers are liable only to the extent of their capital contribution. This is the main reason to convert — but it does not apply retrospectively to obligations incurred by the proprietorship.
Existing contractsLive client contracts, subcontracts, guarantees and insurance policies will need formal novation to the new entity. Build this into the transition plan.
Downstream documentsThe system context file currently forbids PLC concepts. That instruction now inverts: owner capital account becomes share capital, owner drawings become dividends, and board-reserved decisions join owner-reserved ones in the approval matrix. The SRS and the finance analysis need a revision pass.

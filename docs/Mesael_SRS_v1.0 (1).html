1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This document specifies the functional and non-functional requirements for the Mesael Construction Operations Platform. It is the contractual definition of scope: anything described here is in scope; anything not described here is a change request.

It is written from the requirements engineering engagement conducted on site during the week of 27–31 July 2026, and from the design presented to Mesael Construction on 31 July 2026.

1.2 Scope

The system replaces the current combination of Telegram groups, phone calls, paper proformas, Excel sheets, physical box files, and delayed Peachtree entry with a single platform in which a business transaction is entered once and carries its own project, budget, documents, approvals, delivery confirmation, payment, and accounting entry through its whole life.

Twelve modules are in scope. They are listed in Section 2.2 and specified in Section 5.

Out of scope for this release:

  • Replacement of Peachtree as the statutory ledger (integration only — see Q8, Section 10)
  • Client-facing or supplier-facing external portals
  • Building Information Modelling (BIM), CAD, or structural design tooling
  • Automated bank payment initiation (the system prepares; a human executes at the bank)
  • Biometric or hardware attendance devices
  • Any decision-making authority — the system records and enforces human decisions, it does not make them

2. Overall description

2.1 Product perspective

Mesael is an Ethiopian construction sole proprietorship, not a PLC. There are no shareholders, no share capital, no dividends, no board. The Owner/CEO is the legal proprietor and the ultimate authority. The system must reflect this and must not introduce corporate governance concepts that do not apply. C

The company operates as an informal hub-and-spoke organisation centred on the Owner. Every disbursement, every price, and every major relationship passes through him personally. This is the company's principal operational bottleneck and its single point of failure. C

The system's purpose is to correct that operating model — not to reproduce the current Telegram-and-paper process on a screen.

3. As-is process baseline

3.1 The material request journey today

As described by the people who perform each step. C

Step What happens Who On what
1 Site needs material Site engineer / John Telegram
2 Specification checked, priced, site chased for missing detail Firehiwot Telegram, phone
3 Sealed proformas collected — 3–4 standard, up to 20 for unfamiliar machinery Samuel Physical supplier visits
4 Sealed envelopes opened together Samuel + Firehiwot Paper
5 Prices compared, payment request prepared Leta Excel, paper
6 Approval sought → Mesael and Dembi Telegram, phone call, or a signature
7 Money moves Leta RTGS, cheque, or petty cash
8 Goods delivered to site, receipt confirmed Samuel Telegram group
9 Receipts, agreements, vouchers arrive afterwards Firehiwot, Leta Box files 1–7
10 Books catch up — posted from bank statements, weeks or months later Kalkidan Peachtree
11 Site receipts typed into Excel, aggregated, declared online Yamrot Excel, Revenues Authority portal

The observation that drives the whole design: the same request is written by hand at least six times — in a Telegram message, on a paper request, on a proforma, on a payment voucher, in a box file, and finally in Peachtree. Every re-typing is a chance for the number to change, and there is no single place where anyone can see where a request currently stands.

3.2 Current tool landscape

Telegram groups · phone calls · paper proformas · Excel sheets · box files 1–7 · Peachtree · cheques and petty cash · physical bank visits · reception notebook

Nine separate tools doing the work of one system.

3.3 Identified gaps

# Gap Required behaviour in the new system
G1 A request can arrive with no budget behind it Every request is raised against a budget line, with the remaining balance visible before submission
G2 Approvals travel by chat, call and signature One approval queue per person, with a permanent record of who approved what and when
G3 The same quote gets collected twice One procurement channel; one owner per request, visible from the moment it is raised
G4 Good suppliers get worn out by repeated proforma requests Approved vendor list with agreed prices, so routine items need no fresh quote
G5 Money often moves before the documents arrive Supporting documents attached before approval. No document, no approval
G6 The books are written weeks after the fact The accounting entry is produced from the same record that carried the request, at the moment of payment
G7 Vehicles and machinery are tracked by memory Register per vehicle and machine, with hours, mileage, service history, and rental end-date warnings
G8 Stock is not reconciled Goods received, transferred, issued and consumed recorded against the project so the figures reconcile
G9 Good work is invisible in the data Objective operational indicators — requests handled, deliveries on time, documents complete

4. Core design principle — the transaction spine

Every operational transaction travels this ten-stage lifecycle as one living record. It is not re-created at each desk.

Stage Owner
1 Tender and estimate Mesael
2 Client contract Mesael, Dembi
3 Project budget and BOQ John, Firehiwot
4 The request — material, subcontract, rental or expense, raised against a budget line Site, Firehiwot
5 Budget check and approval — the budget effect is shown before anyone approves Dembi, Mesael
6 Purchase or contract — one channel, quotes compared in the system Samuel
7 Delivery or measurement — goods received, or work measured and inspected John, site engineers
8 Payment — released against documents already attached Leta
9 Accounting and tax — posted from the record, not retyped from a bank statement Kalkidan, Yamrot
10 Cost, profit and reporting — live, per project Mesael, Dembi

SPINE-01 [Must] Every transaction record shall carry, at minimum: project and site; client contract; BOQ item or WBS activity; budget line and cost code; requester; classification (material / service / subcontract / equipment); supporting documents; full approval history; supplier or payee; amount, currency and tax treatment; delivery, work or usage confirmation; payment reference; and accounting reference.

SPINE-02 [Must] Data shall be entered once and shall move through the full lifecycle without manual re-creation in Telegram, paper, Excel, or Peachtree.

SPINE-03 [Must] Any user with appropriate permission shall be able to open a record and see its complete current state and history without contacting another person by phone or Telegram.

The system is expected to support:

  • Ethiopian Birr; Africa/Addis_Ababa timestamps; Ethiopian and Gregorian dates
  • Ethiopian fiscal and tax periods
  • Unicode for Amharic, Afaan Oromo, and English
  • Configurable Ethiopian tax types, rates, thresholds, forms, and certificates
  • Employment tax and pension processing
  • Tax-compliant invoice and receipt references
  • Contractor licence category, grade, qualification evidence, and expiry
  • Professional certificate and equipment evidence
  • Tax clearances
  • Public-project securities and guarantees where applicable
  • Labour, safety, quality, environmental, and document-retention records
  • Low-bandwidth and mobile site access
  • Delayed synchronisation / offline capture where site connectivity requires it
  • Secure backups, access control, and immutable audit history

This section is a design and professional-verification checklist, not legal advice.
